Lot & Haul is a single-player strategy/management game about buying abandoned storage lots, making risky appraisals, hauling home the best finds, and selling them before your bad guesses catch up with you.

This page is a development hub for the early alpha: what is playable now, what is being worked on, how updates are likely to arrive, and where the game is headed. The build is still rough, but the core loop is already taking shape, and feedback from real players is the most useful thing for the next round of decisions.

If you like games about uncertain value, inventory pressure, slow-burn progression, and the joy of turning dusty junk into rent money, this is the place to follow along.

What Is Lot & Haul?

You travel to storage-lot auctions, inspect what you can, bid before you know the whole truth, and pack your winnings into a limited cargo grid. Back home, you research the items you bought, improve their condition, authenticate suspicious pieces, and sell to customers with specific tastes.

The heart of the game is incomplete information. An item can look ordinary, promising, broken, fake, or strangely valuable depending on what clues you have uncovered. Appraised value is your best guess. Verified value is the truth. The fun is deciding how much money, cargo space, and time you are willing to risk before certainty arrives.

The long-term goal is a cozy but tactical resale loop: auctions, clues, cargo, storage, repairs, customers, vehicles, progression, and eventually more characterful people and places around the buying and selling economy.

What Is Playable Now

  • Choose a location and browse available storage lots.
  • Spend limited inspection points to reveal item clues before bidding.
  • Enter auctions against NPC price pressure and decide when to bid, fold, or walk away.
  • Pack won items into a vehicle cargo grid.
  • Return home with your haul and send items into storage.
  • Repair, restore, or authenticate items to improve condition or reveal hidden truth.
  • Sell items to nightly customers by matching demand tags and fitting items into their car grids.
  • Choose conservative or aggressive selling strategies.
  • Earn money, improve attributes, buy vehicles, and prepare for the next run.

This is enough to play the main loop from run start to home settlement and selling, but it is still an alpha. Expect placeholder visuals, uneven pacing, rough balance, incomplete onboarding, and systems that may change as the game finds its shape.

Current Development Status

The current alpha is focused on proving the core loop: inspect, bid, haul, research, sell, upgrade, repeat. The most important question right now is not whether every future feature exists, but whether the moment-to-moment decisions are understandable and interesting.

The strongest parts of the build are the information-risk loop, the storage research layer, the customer selling flow, and the foundation for clue-driven item generation. The roughest parts are presentation, onboarding, content density, balance, and the amount of placeholder art still in the build.

The next major polish pass is about making the game easier to read: better item cards, clearer clue displays, more helpful tutorial flow, cleaner UI feedback, and more coherent visual assets. Once the loop is easier to understand, content expansion becomes much more valuable.

Development Rhythm

Devlog Index

Alpha Core Loop

The first public alpha focuses on getting the full loop playable: choose a lot, inspect uncertain items, bid, pack the haul, return home, research the truth, and sell to customers.

Inspection, Clues, And Hidden Value

Item value is built around clues. Surface clues help players form an appraisal before bidding, while hidden clues can change the truth later through authentication. This is the central information-asymmetry fantasy.

Storage And Research

Storage is where the run turns into strategy. Items keep their identity after the auction, surface clues become clearer, and repair, restoration, and authentication determine what is worth keeping, fixing, or selling.

Customer Selling

Selling is built around nightly customers instead of a simple quick-sell button. Customers want certain kinds of items, have limited car space, and give the player a choice between safer and riskier sale outcomes.

Tutorial And Onboarding

The game needs to explain the loop without smothering it. Current work aims to guide players through the first run and hub flow clearly enough that they can keep playing on their own.

Item Generation And Content Growth

The item system is moving toward clue and affix combinations so that more item variety can come from reusable building blocks. This should help future content grow faster without every item being hand-authored from scratch.

Visual Identity And Presentation

The current build uses many placeholder and rough AI-assisted images. Future visual work should make items, cars, storage, locations, and UI feel more coherent while keeping the alpha honest about what is still unfinished.

Roadmap

Roadmap items are directions, not promises. Priorities may change based on playtest feedback, production reality, and which improvements make the game more understandable and fun.

Now

  • Improve first-run onboarding and tutorial clarity.
  • Make inspection, bidding, cargo, storage, and selling easier to understand without external explanation.
  • Rework item presentation so value, condition, clue state, cargo shape, and hidden uncertainty are readable at a glance.
  • Tune economy, inspection costs, customer demand, attribute progression, and vehicle progression.
  • Fix bugs and edge cases that interrupt the core loop.
  • Replace the roughest placeholder visuals with more coherent item, location, vehicle, and warehouse imagery.

Next

  • Add more locations, lots, and item variety.
  • Expand clue coverage and balance hidden-value outcomes.
  • Clarify rarity, naming, and item-generation rules so the game produces more satisfying surprises.
  • Improve vehicle identity with clearer visuals and more meaningful progression.
  • Add convenience tools that reduce repeated clicking during testing, then decide whether any belong in the player-facing game.
  • Add a second buying mode, such as garage-sale style purchases where items are visible up front and price is negotiated rather than won through lot bidding.

Later

  • Deepen customer NPCs with recurring personalities, demand patterns, reputation, scam risk, or selling-related progression.
  • Add rival bidders with preferences, habits, and stronger auction personality.
  • Build an expert/contact network for appraisals, advice, authentication, and special opportunities.
  • Explore collection or prestige systems, such as a museum or showcase for rare verified finds.
  • Add calendar events, special auctions, weekly orders, or time-based goals that shape preparation.
  • Build a more polished experience with music, stronger onboarding, a memorable early-game arc, and platform-level presentation.

What Feedback Helps Most

  • Where did you first feel confused?
  • Did inspection give you enough information to make an interesting bid?
  • Did the difference between appraised value and verified value feel exciting, frustrating, or unclear?
  • Did cargo packing feel like a meaningful constraint?
  • Did customer selling feel like strategy, busywork, or both?
  • Which part most needs polish first: UI clarity, balance, pacing, art, tutorial, or bugs?
  • What decision felt best during a run?

Supporter Note

Following or supporting Lot & Haul means helping shape the game while it is still flexible. The most valuable support is attention: playing builds, reporting confusion, naming what felt good, and pointing out which rough edges made the loop harder to enjoy.
